# Owner pain points, ranked for sellability

An industry research pass (about 45 searches, Australia first) found 10 owner pain points that a
clinic would pay to solve. A product-owner review then ranked them. **The finding:** owners don't
struggle for lack of a dashboard. They struggle at moments ("what will this cost?", "is this
urgent?", "what did the vet say?", "am I giving the meds right?"), and each moment has a dollar line
for the clinic. Today's tiles touch only three of these moments, and weakly. That's why they're
hard to sell.

Figures come from search snippets of named sources. *Sponsor* marks vendor or industry data, and
*weak* marks small or unverified studies.

## Ranking

Weighted score = 2×Sellability + Owner pain + 2×Clinic ROI speed + Differentiation + Feasibility
+ Data value (maximum 40). Sellability and ROI speed count double because "hard to sell" is the
problem to fix.

| Rank | Pain point | Sell | Pain | ROI | Diff | Feas | Data | Total | Deciding factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost surprise and distrust of price | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | **37** | ROI speed: every accepted estimate is visible money within weeks |
| 2 | Giving meds, refills and preventives | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | **31** | Recurring pharmacy revenue at risk to online pharmacies |
| 3 | Forgetting what the vet said, waiting for results | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | **30** | Low risk, fast time savings; reuses #1's clinic-sent cards |
| 4 | "Should I go to the vet?", especially after hours | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | **30** | Feasibility: triage carries liability, and vets will be wary |
| 5 | New puppy or kitten overwhelm | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | **28** | Slower ROI: only starts when a new pet arrives |
| 6 | Missing slow decline in senior and chronic pets | 3 | 4 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 5 | **27** | ROI speed: best data moat, but revenue takes quarters to show |
| 7 | Can't pay a big bill (insurance and payment gap) | 3 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 | **25** | Differentiation: GapOnly, VetPay and Afterpay own it; advice risk |
| 8 | Can't get an appointment | 3 | 4 | 3 | 1 | 4 | 2 | **23** | Differentiation: Vetstoria and Vello own booking |
| 9 | End-of-life decisions | 2 | 5 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 2 | **22** | Sellability: hard to sell on dollars, and ethically delicate |
| 10 | Weight and nutrition confusion | 2 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 3 | **21** | Sellability: weakest dollar story, evidence from small studies |

## The 10, with evidence and our answer

**1. Cost surprise and distrust of price.**
- *Evidence:*
  - 12% of Australian owners needed financial help for vet care, 18% of families.
  - About 1 in 6 deferred check-ups or vaccines, or cut medication.
  - 30% of dog owners who cut visits named affordability (AMA 2025).
  - 36% of UK owners only learn the price at reception (Which?).
  - Only 29% got written pricing before non-routine treatment (CMA).
  - Cost came up in just 23.4% of 917 videoed consults (Groves 2022).
- *Answer:* an **estimate inbox**. The clinic sends a ranged ezyVet estimate. The owner sees plain
  lines, optional against recommended, what the plan covers and instalment options. *Accept*, *Ask
  a question* and *Ask for a cheaper option* each become a proposal (AG-4). The assistant explains
  lines and never judges clinical need.
- *Clinic money:* treatments accepted instead of declined. It also gets ahead of CMA-style price
  transparency.

**2. Giving meds, refills and preventives.**
- *Evidence:*
  - 50.7% of cat owners are rarely or never shown how to medicate; 79% had tablets spat out; only
    41.8% of those who struggle tell the vet (Taylor 2022, n=2,507).
  - Owners bought 6.1 of 12 months of flea and tick cover.
  - Pharmacy is about 19% of practice revenue (US benchmark).
  - Australian online pharmacies sell repeats in "a couple of clicks".
- *Answer:* a **med log** for vet-prescribed medicines only:
  - a schedule from ezyVet, with "given" and "missed" taps;
  - a "running low" alert that becomes a refill proposal;
  - a "how to give it" video chosen by the clinic;
  - a "struggling" button that becomes a note to the vet;
  - no dosing advice.
- *Clinic money:* refills kept at the clinic.

**3. Forgetting what the vet said, waiting for results.**
- *Evidence:*
  - Owner and clinician agreed on discharge instructions only 68.9% of the time, worst on side
    effects and confinement (JVIM 2024).
  - About 60% google test results they don't understand.
  - 53.8% would pay for more frequent updates (Kogan 2019).
- *Answer:* a **visit recap** approved by the clinic, a discharge checklist ("confinement day 3 of
  14"), a "results ready" card with the vet's comment, and a question box that becomes a note.
- *Clinic money:* fewer callbacks, and follow-ups that actually get booked.

**4. "Should I go to the vet?"**
- *Evidence:*
  - Owners under-rated urgency in 28.4% of cases; owners who regularly contact their practice
    judged better (RVC, n=1,772).
  - Up to 67% can't identify emergencies.
  - Only 20% of Australian owners know vet telehealth exists (VetChat, *sponsor*).
- *Answer:* extend AG-3 into a clinic-branded red/amber/green **"Should I come in?"** routing, with
  no diagnosis.
  - *Amber:* pre-fills tomorrow's booking.
  - *Green:* "send a note and photo for the morning".
  - *After hours:* shows the clinic's emergency partner.
- *Clinic money:* VetChat routes owners away from the clinic; this routes them back.

**5. New puppy or kitten overwhelm.**
- *Evidence:*
  - 1 in 5 Australian owners is a first-timer (AMA).
  - 96.7% of pandemic-puppy owners reported a problem behaviour by 21 months (RVC).
  - Behaviour caused 33.7% of deaths in dogs under 3 (Boyd 2018).
- *Answer:* a clinic-branded **first 90 days** pathway: vaccine course, desexing, preventives,
  weekly "normal or not" guides, puppy class and nurse checks, and plan sign-up at peak motivation.

**6. Missing slow decline in senior and chronic pets.**
- *Evidence:*
  - 90% of cats over 12 show osteoarthritis on X-ray, but it's recorded in only 4% of records.
  - 27-28% of owners think the signs are normal ageing (Zoetis, *sponsor*).
  - Only about 14% of seniors get their recommended screening (AAHA, older study).
- *Answer:* keep the weekly check-in for senior and chronic pets only. Use validated mobility
  screens (a feline osteoarthritis checklist, CBPI or LOAD for dogs), share the trend with the vet,
  and produce a "bring this to your visit" summary. This is the year-2 premium module and the data
  moat.

**7. Can't pay a big bill.**
- *Evidence:*
  - 76% of dog owners and 86% of cat owners are uninsured (AMA).
  - Uninsured dogs with bloat (GDV) were 7.4 times more likely to be euthanised before surgery
    that saves 80-90% of them (Boller 2020, 24 Australian emergency centres).
  - GapOnly runs in 1,000+ Australian clinics.
- *Answer:* a light version inside #1: typical emergency costs at this clinic, the pet's insurance
  status and the plan's emergency benefits, plus a one-tap claim-pack request. We take no payments.

**8. Can't get an appointment.**
- *Evidence:*
  - 36.8% of vet vacancies take 12 months or more to fill (AVA 2023/24).
  - 58% of owners have had trouble booking, and those owners are about 3 times as likely to switch
    clinic (Vetstoria, *sponsor*).
- *Answer:* not a product. Add "right-sized" requests (recheck, repeat script, nurse weigh-in)
  inside #3 and #4.

**9. End-of-life decisions.**
- *Evidence:*
  - Guilt is common whether owners decide early or late (Groningen, n=1,542).
  - Guilt is worse without clear information (Padua).
  - 4 in 10 of Pawssum's home visits are euthanasia (*sponsor*).
- *Answer, later:* an owner-scored quality-of-life journal that never draws a conclusion, plus a
  "talk to the vet about next steps" proposal.

**10. Weight and nutrition.**
- *Evidence:*
  - About 41% of dogs and 32% of cats are overweight.
  - Owners underestimate body condition in about 43-54% of cases (*weak*).
  - Nutrition is the top question owners ask AI, but 71% trust their vet most (*sponsor*).
- *Answer:* a field inside the med and chronic programs, not a tile of its own.

## What we built

We chose to build **#1, #3, #4 and #5** first. #2 (medication log) is next on the roadmap.

| Pain point | Built as | Requirement IDs |
|---|---|---|
| #1 Cost surprise | **Costs**: the estimate inbox plus the year ahead (the old 12-month forecast folded in) | CO-1 to CO-4, HT-4 |
| #3 Forgetting what the vet said | **Last visit**: the recap, instructions with day counts, results, follow-up and questions | VR-1 to VR-3 |
| #4 "Should I go to the vet?" | **Something not right?**: call now, within 24 hours, or keep an eye on it | TR-1 to TR-5 |
| #5 New pet overwhelm | **First 90 days**: vaccine course, home steps, weekly tip, young-pet plan | NP-1, NP-2 |
| (kept) Slow decline | **Weekly check-in**, senior pets only | HT-1 to HT-3 |

Retired from the earlier dashboard: parasite cover (to come back inside the medication log) and
the weight tile (weight stays an assistant action).

## Hero bundle and the pilot metric

**Pitch to a clinic owner:** "Turn the estimates you already write, the scripts you already
prescribe and the discharge talks you already give into accepted treatment, refills kept at your
clinic and fewer callbacks. It's branded as your clinic, and the owner confirms before anything is
sent."

**90-day pilot metric:** estimate acceptance rate (dollars accepted ÷ dollars estimated) against
the clinic's own previous 90 days in ezyVet. Secondary metrics: refill retention and
recap-related callbacks.

**Caveats:**
- Many figures are sponsor-funded or come from small studies.
- #1 depends on reading ezyVet estimates, which has to be checked against `docs/ezyvet.md`. The
  CSV export is the fallback for pilots.
